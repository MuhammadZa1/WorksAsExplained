Fixing a Phone That's Overheating or Draining Fast

7 min read · By Muhammad Zakria

A phone that gets warm during heavy use (navigation with the screen on, video calls, gaming) is normal and not something to worry about. A phone that's hot doing nothing, or that's suddenly draining far faster than usual, points to something specific worth tracking down.

Step 1: Identify what's actually running

Check the battery usage breakdown (Settings → Battery on both platforms) for the period when you noticed the heat or drain. A single app using a disproportionate share of battery relative to how much you actually used it is the most common cause — often something stuck in a loop or a background process that isn't behaving as intended.

Common software causes

  • A stuck app or process — force-close the suspicious app and see if the phone cools down within a few minutes.
  • A pending or failed software update — sometimes downloads or installs in the background and runs hot until it finishes or fails; checking for updates and letting one complete can resolve this.
  • Excessive background location or sync activity — see our guide on managing app permissions for reducing this specifically.
  • Malware — much less common than the above, but a phone that's persistently hot with high data usage and battery drain you can't attribute to any single app is worth taking seriously; see our guide on spotting malicious apps.

Charging-related heat

Phones commonly get warm while charging, especially with fast charging or while actively being used during a charge (like navigating while plugged in). This is normal thermal behavior, not a fault. If a phone gets unusually hot while charging even when not in use, try a different cable and charger before assuming the phone itself is at fault — damaged cables and non-certified chargers are common causes.

Environmental heat is a real factor

Direct sunlight, a hot car, or a phone case that traps heat can all push a phone into a protective thermal state, sometimes dimming the screen or slowing performance until it cools. This is the phone protecting its battery and components, not a malfunction — move it out of direct heat and let it cool naturally rather than trying to force it to keep working at full speed.

When to actually worry

A phone that's hot to the touch while sitting idle and unused, especially paired with visible battery swelling (a case that no longer sits flat, a screen lifting slightly at the edges) is a genuine hardware concern — stop using and charging it, and get it looked at by a professional rather than continuing to use it.

If a specific app reliably causes overheating every time you open it, check whether an update is available, and if the problem persists after updating, that's worth reporting to the developer directly — you're likely not the only one experiencing it.

Troubleshooting order

  • Check the battery usage breakdown for a specific culprit app.
  • Force-close and update anything suspicious.
  • Try a different cable/charger if the heat happens specifically while charging.
  • Move the phone out of direct heat and let it cool naturally.
  • Treat idle overheating paired with battery swelling as a hardware issue needing professional attention.

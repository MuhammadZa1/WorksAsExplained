Taming Notifications So Your Phone Stops Interrupting You

6 min read · By Muhammad Zakria

Notification overload usually isn't one app's fault — it's dozens of apps each claiming a small, "reasonable" amount of your attention, which adds up to a phone that buzzes constantly. A one-time cleanup fixes most of it.

Start with a full audit, not app by app

Both platforms have a central notifications settings screen (Settings → Notifications on iPhone; Settings → Notifications → App notifications on Android) listing every app with notification permission in one place. Go through the full list once, rather than reacting to individual annoying notifications as they come — it's faster and you won't miss anything.

Sort apps into three groups

  • Needs to interrupt you immediately — calls, messages from people, calendar reminders. Leave these as full, active notifications.
  • Useful, but not urgent — shipping updates, app updates, most work tools. Consider muting sound and banners but keeping them visible in your notification list, or scheduling a "summary" delivery if your platform supports it.
  • Not needed at all — most game notifications, promotional pushes, "someone you follow posted" alerts. Turn these off entirely.

Use scheduled summaries for the middle group

iPhone's "Scheduled Summary" and Android's similar digest features bundle lower-priority notifications into a batch delivered a few times a day instead of individually throughout the day. This is the best option for apps that have genuinely useful notifications you don't want to lose entirely, just not immediately.

Turn off badges and lock-screen previews for noisy apps

Even when a notification isn't loud, a constant badge count or lock-screen preview creates a background pull to check your phone. For apps you've decided don't need immediate attention, disabling the badge and lock-screen preview (while still allowing the notification to exist in your list) reduces the temptation to check without fully turning the app's alerts off.

Focus modes and Do Not Disturb, used deliberately

Both platforms support custom focus/DND profiles that allow only specific contacts or app types through during set hours (work hours, sleep, family time). Setting this up once — rather than manually toggling Do Not Disturb on and off — means the right people can still reach you while everything else waits.

Revisit after a new app install

Many apps request notification permission during onboarding with an enticing prompt. Get in the habit of saying no by default when installing something new, and only enabling notifications later if you find you actually want them — it's easier to turn something on when you miss it than to unwind dozens of unwanted notification habits later.

If you're doing this cleanup on a shared family phone or a device used by both work and personal accounts, go through it separately for each major use case — a notification pattern that's fine for personal use might be genuinely disruptive during work hours, and vice versa.

Quick checklist

  • Do one full pass through the central notifications settings screen.
  • Sort apps into urgent, useful-but-not-urgent, and unnecessary.
  • Use scheduled summaries for the middle group.
  • Turn off badges and previews for apps you're not fully muting.
  • Set up a focus/DND profile for predictable quiet periods.
  • Default new app installs to notifications off, and opt in later if needed.

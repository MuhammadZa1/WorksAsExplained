Set Up a Wireless Printer Without the Usual Fight

7 min read · By Muhammad Zakria

Wireless printers have a reputation for being unreliable, but most connection failures trace back to one of three specific causes. Fix those in order and setup usually takes under ten minutes, including the print test.

Step 1: Put the printer on the right network band

This is the single most common cause of "the printer won't connect." Many printers only support the 2.4GHz Wi-Fi band, not 5GHz — but plenty of routers broadcast both under the same network name. If your phone or laptop connects to 5GHz by default, the printer may simply be unable to see the network it's trying to join. Check your router settings for a separate 2.4GHz network name, or temporarily split the bands into two different network names during setup.

Step 2: Set up the printer before installing software

Get the printer itself connected to your Wi-Fi first, using its own screen or control panel, before touching your computer. Most printers have a setup wizard in their menu — look for "Wireless Setup" or "Network Settings" — that lets you select your network and enter the Wi-Fi password directly on the printer. Confirm it shows a connected status before moving to the next step.

Step 3: Let your computer find it automatically

On Windows, go to Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Printers & scanners and click "Add device" — once the printer is on the network, it usually appears without needing a driver download. On Mac, go to System Settings > Printers & Scanners and click the plus button. Only download manufacturer software if the printer doesn't appear automatically after a minute or two.

Skip the manufacturer's bundled software suite unless you specifically need a feature like scanning to a particular app. Those installers often bundle extra utilities, trial software, and background processes you don't need just to print — your operating system's built-in driver support handles basic printing fine.

Step 4: If it still won't connect, restart in this order

Turn off the printer, then restart your router, then turn the printer back on once the router has fully rebooted (give it a minute or two). This clears stale network assignments that are a common, invisible cause of connection failures, particularly after a power outage or router restart where devices reconnect out of sync.

Step 5: Keep the connection stable long-term

If the printer worked at setup but regularly drops off the network, check whether it's gone to sleep and lost its network address, or whether your router periodically reassigns IP addresses in a way the printer doesn't handle well. Most routers let you reserve a fixed IP address for a specific device in their DHCP settings — reserving one for your printer prevents this category of problem entirely.

A quick setup order

  • Confirm the printer is joining a 2.4GHz network, not 5GHz-only.
  • Connect the printer to Wi-Fi using its own control panel first.
  • Add it through your operating system's built-in printer settings.
  • Only install manufacturer software if it doesn't appear automatically.
  • Restart printer then router, in that order, if connection issues persist.
  • Reserve a fixed IP address for the printer to prevent future drops.
